Frequently Asked Questions

What do I do if a ball gets stuck or other game malfunction?

If you encounter a stuck ball or other issue while playing, raise your hand and wait for a scorekeeper or tournament official to come assist you.

DO NOT leave your game unattended and please trap the ball up as necessary until you are assisted.

How long should the main tournament take?

During qualifying, you can expect around 45 minutes per round. The number of rounds could vary depending on player performance and the points they are accumulating each round. Qualifying ends once 16 players reach the target score.

During Finals, each round takes approximately 2 hours. Depending on how far into the finals you advance, be mindful that it could take a majority of your Sunday during show hours.

How does qualifying work for the main tournament?

Qualifying will be split into two sessions of target match-play; one session Friday and one session on Saturday.

Target match-play consists of playing in a group of 4 (or 3 if necessary) each round. At the end of your round, you will receive points corresponding to your finishing position in that round. You accumulate points until you reach a target score. Once the target score is reached, your qualifying is complete.

If you are one of the first 16 players in a qualifying session to reach the target score, you will qualifying for finals on Sunday.

If you are not ready to play your game in a given round, your balls will be plunged by the tournament director! If you miss an entire round, you will be removed from the tournament.

Once you complete your game, stay at the machine, raise your hand, and wait for a scorekeeper to come record your scores.

If you encounter a stuck ball or other issue while playing, raise your hand and wait for a scorekeeper or tournament official to come assist you.

How do the finals work for the main tournament?

The top 16 players from EACH qualifying session will advance to the finals, for a total field of 32 players.

Finals will be played in a match-play format with 3 games per round and 4-2-1-0 scoring.

In each round, groups of 4 players will play a 4-game match. After each match, the top score on a machine will receive 4 points, 2nd place 2 points, 3rd place 1 point, and last place zero points.

At the end of the match, the two players with the most points will advance to the next round and the bottom two will be eliminated. In case two or more players in the same group finish with the same amount of points and two players haven’t advanced from that group (i.e., there is a tie-break to advance), a tie-breaking game will be played on a random machine selected by the software.

Final rankings will be determined by the number of points obtained in the round that the player was eliminated, plus the result of the advancing tie-breaker, and non-advancing ties will not be broken.
